## Glaucoma

**Definition:** Group of optic neuropathies characterised by progressive retinal ganglion cell loss and visual field defects, often associated with elevated IOP.

**Types:**
- Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) — most common; painless, insidious onset
- Acute angle-closure glaucoma (AACG) — painful, sudden vision loss, fixed mid-dilated pupil; emergency
- Normal-tension glaucoma (NTG) — optic nerve damage despite IOP 10-21 mmHg
- Secondary glaucomas — pigment dispersion, pseudoexfoliation, neovascular, steroid-induced

**IOP:** Normal 10-21 mmHg. Target IOP set individually based on baseline and damage severity.

**Optic disc signs:** Increased cup-to-disc ratio (>0.6 suspicious), disc haemorrhages, notching of neuroretinal rim (inferior > superior > nasal > temporal — ISNT rule reversed in glaucoma), peripapillary atrophy.

**Investigations:** Visual field (Humphrey 24-2), OCT RNFL, gonioscopy, pachymetry (thin cornea → underestimates IOP).

**Treatment:**
- First-line: Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost, bimatoprost) — once daily, evening
- Second-line: Beta-blockers (timolol), alpha-agonists (brimonidine), carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (dorzolamide)
- Laser: SLT (selective laser trabeculoplasty) for POAG; LPI (laser peripheral iridotomy) for angle-closure
- Surgery: Trabeculectomy, MIGS (minimally invasive glaucoma surgery)

**AACG Management:** IV acetazolamide, topical timolol + pilocarpine + apraclonidine, IV mannitol if needed. Definitive: bilateral LPI.

## Retinal Diseases

### Diabetic Retinopathy (DR)
**Stages:**
- NPDR: Microaneurysms → dot-blot haemorrhages → hard exudates → cotton-wool spots → IRMA → venous beading
- PDR: Neovascularisation (NVD/NVE) → vitreous haemorrhage → tractional retinal detachment

**Diabetic Macular Oedema (DMO):** Leading cause of vision loss in DR. Centre-involving DMO: anti-VEGF first-line (ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab).

**Screening:** Annual dilated fundus exam. Singapore: MyDR national screening programme.

**Treatment:**
- NPDR: Optimise HbA1c, BP, lipids
- PDR: Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) or anti-VEGF
- DMO: Anti-VEGF injections (6 loading doses then PRN/T&E)

### Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)
**Dry AMD:** Drusen, RPE atrophy, geographic atrophy. No proven treatment; AREDS2 supplements slow progression.

**Wet AMD:** Choroidal neovascularisation (CNV) — subretinal fluid, haemorrhage, sudden central vision loss.
Treatment: Anti-VEGF (ranibizumab, aflibercept, brolucizumab) — monthly loading then PRN or treat-and-extend.

**Amsler grid:** Metamorphopsia = warning sign of wet AMD.

### Retinal Detachment (RD)
**Types:**
- Rhegmatogenous (most common): Tear → liquefied vitreous enters subretinal space. Risk: myopia, trauma, post-cataract surgery.
- Tractional: Fibrovascular proliferation (diabetic, sickle cell).
- Exudative: No break — tumour, uveitis, Coats disease.

**Symptoms:** Flashes, floaters, curtain/shadow in visual field. Macula-off RD = visual acuity reduced.

**Surgery:** Pneumatic retinopexy, scleral buckle, pars plana vitrectomy (PPV). Macula-on = surgery within 24h.

### Retinal Vascular Occlusions
- **CRVO:** Disc swelling, flame haemorrhages in all 4 quadrants, dilated tortuous veins. Ischaemic vs non-ischaemic.
- **BRVO:** Sectoral flame haemorrhages. Macular oedema: anti-VEGF or laser.
- **CRAO:** Sudden painless vision loss; cherry-red spot on pale retina. Treat within 4.5h (thrombolysis, ocular massage, ACRD protocol). Rule out GCA (ESR, CRP, temporal artery biopsy).

## Corneal Diseases

**Corneal layers (outer to inner):** Epithelium → Bowman's layer → Stroma (90% thickness) → Descemet's membrane → Endothelium.

**Keratoconus:** Progressive corneal ectasia; irregular astigmatism, Fleischer ring, Vogt's striae. Corneal cross-linking (CXL) halts progression. Contact lenses for vision; PKP/DALK if advanced.

**Fuchs Endothelial Dystrophy:** Corneal guttata, endothelial failure, oedema. DMEK (Descemet's membrane endothelial keratoplasty) is gold standard.

**Bacterial Keratitis:** Most common: Pseudomonas (contact lens), Staphylococcus. Ulcer + hypopyon. Intensive topical fluoroquinolones (moxifloxacin). Scrape for culture.

**Herpes Simplex Keratitis:** Dendritic ulcer (fluorescein staining, branching with terminal bulbs). Topical/oral acyclovir. Avoid steroids in epithelial disease.

**Dry Eye Disease:** OSDI score, Schirmer's test, TBUT <10s. Treat: artificial tears, lid hygiene, omega-3, cyclosporine (Restasis), punctal plugs.

## Cataract

**Types:** Nuclear sclerosis (most common, myopic shift), posterior subcapsular (steroids, diabetes — affects near vision early), cortical.

**Indications for surgery:** Visual acuity affecting daily life, or to treat complications (phacomorphic glaucoma, lens-induced uveitis).

**Procedure:** Phacoemulsification + IOL implantation. Complications: posterior capsule rupture, vitreous loss, endophthalmitis, CMO, posterior capsule opacification (PCO — YAG laser capsulotomy).

**Endophthalmitis:** Severe post-op infection. Gram-positive (Staph epidermidis most common). Intravitreal vancomycin + ceftazidime. EVS trial: PPV if LP or worse.

## Clinical Examination

**Visual Acuity:** Snellen chart at 6m. Record as 6/6, 6/12, etc. If <6/60: count fingers (CF), hand movements (HM), perception of light (PL), no perception of light (NPL).

**Slit-lamp:** Always examine in order — lids/lashes → conjunctiva → cornea → anterior chamber (flare/cells) → iris/pupil → lens.

**Fundoscopy:** Red reflex → disc (C:D ratio, rim, vessels) → macula (last, bright light). Dilate with tropicamide 1% ± phenylephrine 2.5%.

**Afferent Pupillary Defect (APD/RAPD):** Swinging flashlight test. Indicates optic nerve or extensive retinal disease.

**IOP measurement:** Goldmann applanation tonometry (gold standard). Correct for central corneal thickness.
